How to read this guide

Each metric below is a self-contained card with the same 7 parts: What it is → How to capture → Baseline → Track → Execution-model lens → Blue/Green & the Green conversion → Watch-outs. Click any card to expand it; use the sticky bar to jump. The cards use the same IDs as the GenAI-Effectiveness-Metrics.xlsx workbook — capture the numbers here, record them there.

Every Green claim in this guide follows one discipline: a Trigger (the metric movement automatically causes an action, not just a report), an Owner (one named person, never "the team"), and a Measure (track what changed because of the action, with a verification date). Efficiency claims without these three stay Blue forever — saved time that nobody re-allocates simply evaporates into everyday comfort. When hours ARE freed, make the allocation decision explicit: Redeploy them (to billable/backlog work), Resize (absorb growth without hiring), or Reinvest (build a new offering).

Level 1 — start now, existing tools Weeks 1-6

S3 Deployment frequency   Level 1 · start now Blue Money

1 · What it is

How often the team puts code into production. It is the "pulse rate" of delivery — frequent, small, boring releases are the hallmark of a healthy pipeline.
10-second example: Team Nova deployed 9 times in June across 4 weeks → 2.25 deploys/week.

2 · How to capture it Example stack: Team Nova (T&M · GitHub Actions) · Owner: Scrum Master · Monthly · Effort: ~10 min

  1. Identify the production deploy workflow (e.g. deploy-prod.yml).
  2. Monthly, count successful runs: Actions → deploy-prod → Status: success → date filter, or CLI: gh run list --workflow deploy-prod --status success --created 2026-06-01..2026-06-30.
  3. Divide by weeks in the period; type into the Baseline Tracker.
  4. Jenkins: count successful builds of the prod job. ADO: Releases → prod stage succeeded count.

3 · Baseline it

Nova pulls 4 months of history before Copilot rollout: 34 successful prod deploys over 17 weeks → baseline = 2.0 deploys/week. Written once into the Baseline column; never edited again.

4 · Track improvements

BaselineQ1Q2Read
Deploys/week2.02.53.0Rising ✓
Change failure rate (pair)15%15%14%Stable ✓

Frequency rose 50% with CFR flat — a genuine, safe speed-up. If CFR had climbed to 22%, the same frequency number would be a warning, not a win. Never read this metric alone.

5 · What it means in each execution model

T&MClient experiences faster feature flow — supports renewals and expansion conversations; more deploys of billable work.
Managed ServicesSmaller, more frequent changes = lower change risk = fewer SLA-threatening incidents.
Fixed PriceFrequent integration protects milestones — big-bang integration is the classic fixed-price killer.
Outcome-BasedEnables the contracted outcomes: you cannot hit uptime/adoption milestones you can't ship toward.

Staff Aug: measure at the client-team level only; an individual's 'deploy count' is meaningless.

6 · Blue Money — and the path to Green

Classification: Blue — context metric.

Alone it stays Blue. It becomes part of a Green story only when paired with change failure rate: "Release cadence up 50% with zero stability loss — features now reach the business in days, not weeks." To make it stick: Trigger — monthly delivery review flags any frequency drop; Owner — Scrum Master; Measure — deploys/week alongside CFR.

🗣 Say it to the client: “We ship 50% more often with no increase in failures — your features reach users faster at the same risk level.”

7 · Watch-outs

  • Gaming risk: Deploying trivial/no-op changes to inflate the count.
  • Counterbalance with: Q1 Change failure rate
  • Common mistake: Comparing frequency across teams with different release models (mobile app store vs web) — trend each team against its own baseline only.
Q1 Change failure rate (CFR)   Level 1 · start now Green Money

1 · What it is

Of everything deployed, what % broke production and needed a hotfix, patch or rollback?
10-second example: Atlas made 20 deployments in June; 3 required emergency fixes → CFR = 15%.

2 · How to capture it Example stack: Team Atlas (Managed Services · ADO + ServiceNow) · Owner: Tech Lead · Monthly · Effort: ~20 min

  1. Adopt one convention: every emergency fix release is tagged hotfix (ADO release tag / work item tag).
  2. Monthly: count total prod deployments (Pipelines → Releases) and hotfix-tagged ones.
  3. Cross-check with ServiceNow: incidents whose 'caused by change' field links to a deployment.
  4. CFR = hotfix or rollback deployments ÷ total deployments.

3 · Baseline it

Atlas reviews 4 months pre-GenAI: 26 deployments, 4 needed hotfixes → baseline CFR = 15.4%. (DORA elite benchmark is ~5% — directional, not pass/fail.)

4 · Track improvements

BaselineQ1 (AI ramp)Q2Read
CFR15.4%18%12%Dip then improve
Deploys/week (pair)1.61.92.2Rising ✓

The Q1 bump is the classic GenAI ramp pattern — more AI code merged before review practices caught up. Atlas responded with an AI-code review checklist; by Q2, CFR beat baseline while shipping more. That recovery story is more credible to a client than a suspiciously perfect line.

5 · What it means in each execution model

T&MHigh CFR erodes client trust and renewal probability — the T&M contract's real currency.
Managed ServicesMost direct money link: each SLA breach costs Atlas ~$25K. CFR is the leading indicator of breaches.
Fixed PriceEvery failed change consumes unpaid rework hours straight out of the fixed-price margin.
Outcome-BasedOften literally a contracted KPI ('zero Sev1 escapes'). CFR movement = payment movement.

6 · Green Money — and the path to Green

Classification: Green — outcome metric.

Convert via the incident cost model agreed with Finance: (avoided failures × cost per failure) + (avoided SLA penalties). Trigger — CFR above baseline for 2 consecutive months auto-opens a review-process action; Owner — Tech Lead; Measure — CFR × agreed $-per-failure, reported quarterly.

🗣 Say it to the client: “Failure rate fell from 15% to 12% while deploying 37% more — that's 3 fewer emergency fixes and ~$75K in penalty exposure avoided this year.”

7 · Watch-outs

  • Gaming risk: Quietly shipping fixes as 'features' to avoid the hotfix tag — reconcile against ITSM incident records monthly.
  • Counterbalance with: S3 Deployment frequency
  • Common mistake: Counting all bugs as change failures. CFR counts only deployment-caused production failures.
Q3 Defect density   Level 1 · start now Green Money

1 · What it is

Production bugs logged per release — the honest counterweight to "we write code faster now."
10-second example: Phoenix shipped 3 releases in June with 11 production bugs → 3.7 defects/release.

2 · How to capture it Example stack: Team Phoenix (Fixed Price · Jira + GitLab) · Owner: QA Lead · Monthly · Effort: ~15 min

  1. Ensure prod bugs carry Environment = Production (or an agreed label) in Jira.
  2. Monthly JQL: project = PHX AND type = Bug AND environment = Production AND created >= startOfMonth(-1).
  3. Divide by releases shipped in the same window.
  4. ADO equivalent: Boards query, Type=Bug, Found-In=Production, date range.

3 · Baseline it

Phoenix, 4 months pre-GenAI: 47 production bugs across 12 releases → baseline = 3.9 defects/release. Also record severity mix (2 critical, 18 major, 27 minor) — a stable count with worsening severity is still a regression.

4 · Track improvements

BaselineQ1Q2Read
Defects/release3.94.64.1Bump, recovering
Cycle time (pair)5.0d4.4d4.2dFaster ✓

Speed improved but defects rose — gains partially borrowed from quality. Phoenix added AI-generated-code test requirements; Q2 shows recovery. If Q3 doesn't return to ≤ baseline, the honest conclusion is that GenAI has not yet paid off on this team — say so, and fix the test safety net first.

5 · What it means in each execution model

T&MClient is billed for defect fixing — rising density means paying twice, a renewal killer.
Managed ServicesDefects convert to incident volume → SLA exposure and support cost.
Fixed PriceWarranty-period defect fixing is free work; every escaped defect is direct margin erosion.
Outcome-BasedFrequently a contracted KPI (defect SLA); density trend predicts contract compliance.

6 · Green Money — and the path to Green

Classification: Green — outcome metric.

Convert via avoided rework: defects avoided × average fix cost (Phoenix measures ~6 engineer-hours per prod defect ≈ $300 at cost). Trigger — density above baseline auto-adds 'strengthen tests for AI code' to the sprint; Owner — QA Lead; Measure — defects/release × fix cost, quarterly.

🗣 Say it to the client: “Quality held: after a controlled ramp, defect density is back at baseline while delivery is 16% faster — speed did not come out of quality.”

7 · Watch-outs

  • Gaming risk: Not logging small prod bugs. Spot-audit support channels/ITSM monthly.
  • Counterbalance with: S1 Cycle time
  • Common mistake: Ignoring severity mix — 4 minor bugs ≠ 4 criticals.
S4 PR throughput (team)   Level 1 · start now Blue Money

1 · What it is

Merged pull requests per engineer per week, reported for the team only — never per person.
10-second example: Nova (8 devs) merged 109 PRs in a 4-week month → 109 ÷ 8 ÷ 4 = 3.4 PRs/eng/week.

2 · How to capture it Example stack: Team Nova (T&M · GitHub) · Owner: Tech Lead · Monthly · Effort: ~5 min

  1. Monthly GitHub search: repo:ust/nova-app is:pr is:merged merged:2026-06-01..2026-06-30 → note the count.
  2. Divide by team size and weeks.
  3. GitLab: Merge request analytics. ADO: Repos → Pull Requests → Completed + date filter.
  4. Publish only the team number. If anyone builds a per-developer league table, stop it — that path leads to gaming and burnout.

3 · Baseline it

Nova pre-GenAI: 3.4 PRs/eng/week (4-month average).

4 · Track improvements

BaselineQ1Q2Read
PRs/eng/week3.44.85.9+74% 🚀
Cycle time (pair)5.2d5.1d5.0dFlat ⚠️
Rework rate (pair)6%7.5%8%Rising ⚠️

This exact pattern is the AI Productivity Paradox (Faros 2025: +98% PRs, delivery flat). Nova generates far more PRs, but end-to-end delivery hasn't moved and rework is creeping up. The correct executive readout is NOT "throughput up 74%!" — it is "code generation is no longer our constraint; review and integration now are."

5 · What it means in each execution model

T&MMore PRs ≠ more billable value. Never show this to a T&M client as productivity — show cycle time instead.
Managed ServicesVolume signal only; watch that ticket-related fixes aren't inflating it.
Fixed PriceUseful internally to see if AI is producing volume; margin case still rests on cycle time and defects.
Outcome-BasedIrrelevant to the client — only contracted KPIs matter. Internal diagnostic only.

Staff Aug: NEVER report per-individual PR counts to the client — it invites exactly the individual-ranking trap the whole framework forbids.

6 · Blue Money — and the path to Green

Classification: Blue — activity metric.

Converts only through pairing: if throughput rises AND cycle time falls AND quality holds, then the Green claim is faster time-to-market (told through S1, not S4). Trigger — throughput up while cycle time flat for 2 quarters → automatic review-bottleneck investigation; Owner — Engineering Manager; Measure — the S4/S1/Q2 triple, together.

🗣 Say it to the client: Internal only. If asked: “Code output is up sharply; we're deliberately investing in review capacity so it converts into delivery speed rather than defects.”

7 · Watch-outs

  • Gaming risk: PR splitting (10 trivial PRs instead of 1 real one). Counter: rework rate + review turnaround.
  • Counterbalance with: S1 Cycle time + E1 DevEx
  • Common mistake: Celebrating this number in a leadership deck without its pairs — the single most common GenAI reporting mistake.
S5 Review turnaround time   Level 1 · start now Blue Money

1 · What it is

How long a PR waits for its first review, and how long until merge. GenAI moves the delivery bottleneck from writing code to reviewing it — this metric watches that door.
10-second example: a Nova PR opened Monday 09:00, first comment Tuesday 11:00 (26h), merged Wednesday 15:00 (54h total).

2 · How to capture it Example stack: Team Nova (T&M · GitHub) · Owner: Tech Lead · Monthly · Effort: ~30 min manual

  1. Monthly, sample 25 merged PRs (every Nth from the merged list — don't cherry-pick).
  2. For each record: opened → first review activity → merged timestamps (all on the PR timeline).
  3. Report the medians. Automate later via API or free tooling if the client permits.
  4. ADO: 'time to first comment' dashboard widgets exist out of the box.

3 · Baseline it

Nova pre-GenAI medians: 14h to first review, 26h to merge.

4 · Track improvements

BaselineQ1Q2 (after fix)Read
First review (median)14h22h12hSpike, then fixed
Open → merge (median)26h38h24hRecovered ✓

Q1: Copilot tripled PR volume and the review queue ballooned — coding-speed gains were being consumed downstream. Nova's fix: CODEOWNERS for auto-routing, a 4-hour first-response working agreement, max-400-line PR guideline, and lint/test bots so humans review only logic. Q2 beat baseline. This fix is usually the single highest-ROI action of the whole GenAI rollout.

5 · What it means in each execution model

T&MHours in review queue are billable hours producing nothing the client can see.
Managed ServicesSlow review of fix-PRs directly worsens MTTR and SLA exposure.
Fixed PriceQueue time is schedule risk — on a fixed deadline, a 3-day review lag compounds brutally.
Outcome-BasedSlows everything the contracted KPIs depend on.

6 · Blue Money — and the path to Green

Classification: Blue — converts via cycle time.

Shrinking the queue converts writing-speed into delivery-speed: the Green story appears in S1. Trigger — median first-review > 24h in any month auto-adds a review-capacity item to the retro; Owner — Tech Lead; Measure — review medians + cycle time.

🗣 Say it to the client: “We found the new bottleneck AI creates — review — and engineered it away; that's why cycle time fell instead of defects rising.”

7 · Watch-outs

  • Gaming risk: Rubber-stamp 'LGTM' approvals make this number look great while quality collapses. Counter: escaped defects.
  • Counterbalance with: Q4 Escaped defects / Q3 Defect density
  • Common mistake: Measuring only to-merge time; the to-first-review lag is where the queue pain actually lives.
A1 License utilization   Level 1 · start now Blue Money

1 · What it is

Of the GenAI seats being paid for, what % see meaningful weekly use? Idle seats are the purest waste in the whole program.
10-second example: the account pays for 200 Copilot seats; the admin dashboard shows 118 active last week → 59% utilization.

2 · How to capture it Example stack: Account level (all teams) · Copilot admin · Owner: Engineering Manager · Monthly · Effort: ~10 min

  1. GitHub org → Settings → Copilot → Access & usage (or the usage API). Zero setup.
  2. Record: seats paid, weekly active users, last-activity dates.
  3. List every seat idle ≥ 30 days by team (not by name in any report).
  4. Same pattern for any other vendor's admin console.

3 · Baseline it

First reading after rollout month 1: 200 seats × $19/mo = $45.6K/yr spend at 59% utilization — i.e. ~$18.7K/yr currently buying nothing.

4 · Track improvements

M1M3M6Read
Utilization59%74%83%Enablement working
Seats paid200200170Right-sized ✓

Months 1-3: fix enablement (training, IDE setup clinics, champions per team). Month 6: seats that stayed idle were surrendered at renewal — 30 seats × $19 × 12 = $6.8K/yr hard saving, and the remaining spend now buys real usage.

5 · What it means in each execution model

T&MTool cost usually sits in UST's margin — idle seats burn it directly.
Managed ServicesSame: seat cost is inside the fixed fee; utilization is pure margin hygiene.
Fixed PriceBudgeted as project cost — idle seats inflate the bid or eat the margin.
Outcome-BasedAny spend not moving a contracted KPI is dead weight; utilization is the first check.

6 · Blue Money — and the path to Green

Classification: Blue — with a DIRECT Green conversion.

The cleanest conversion in the catalog: Trigger — any seat idle 60 days is auto-flagged for reallocation or surrender at renewal; Owner — Engineering Manager; Measure — license spend vs utilization, quarterly. The cancelled-seat invoice delta is auditable Green Money Finance accepts without argument.

🗣 Say it to the client: “We actively manage AI spend: utilization is 83% and we cut unused seats — you're not paying for shelfware.”

7 · Watch-outs

  • Gaming risk: 'Active' can mean one autocomplete a week. Use engaged-use definitions where the vendor offers them.
  • Counterbalance with: A2 AI-assisted PR share (depth, not just logins)
  • Common mistake: Publishing idle-user names — this is a budget metric, not a surveillance tool.
I2 Sev1/Sev2 incidents   Level 1 · start now Green Money

1 · What it is

Customer-impacting production incidents per quarter — the stability number executives already track, now watched for GenAI side-effects in both directions.
10-second example: Atlas logged 6 Sev1+Sev2 incidents last quarter; two were traced to rushed AI-generated changes.

2 · How to capture it Example stack: Team Atlas (Managed Services · ServiceNow) · Owner: Delivery Manager · Quarterly · Effort: ~20 min

  1. Quarterly ServiceNow report: Severity IN (1,2) AND assignment_group = Atlas AND opened_at in quarter.
  2. Tag root cause: was a recent deployment involved? Was AI-generated code implicated (from the PR tag)?
  3. Record count + total war-room hours per incident.

3 · Baseline it

Atlas, 4 quarters pre-GenAI: 5-7 per quarter, average 6. Finance-agreed cost per incident: ~$12K (war-room hours + management time + penalty exposure).

4 · Track improvements

BaselineQ1Q2Read
Sev1+Sev2 / qtr674Improving after ramp
Deploys/week (pair)1.61.92.2Shipping more ✓

Q2 tells a strong story: one-third fewer major incidents while deploying 37% more. The pairing matters — fewer incidents achieved by freezing deployments would be a failure dressed as success.

5 · What it means in each execution model

T&MIncidents consume hours the client resents paying for; stability sells renewals.
Managed ServicesTHE money metric: incidents drive penalty payouts and margin-destroying firefighting inside a fixed fee.
Fixed PriceWar-room time is unbudgeted free work straight from margin.
Outcome-BasedUsually contracted directly (uptime %, zero-Sev1 clauses) — incident count ≈ invoice amount.

6 · Green Money — and the path to Green

Classification: Green — outcome metric.

(Baseline − actual) × $12K per incident + penalties avoided. Trigger — any AI-implicated Sev1 auto-triggers a review-gate retrospective; Owner — Delivery Manager; Measure — quarterly incident cost vs baseline, signed by Finance.

🗣 Say it to the client: “Two fewer major incidents per quarter at higher deploy volume = ~$96K/yr avoided cost and zero SLA penalties this year.”

7 · Watch-outs

  • Gaming risk: Severity downgrading (calling a Sev2 a Sev3). Audit classification quarterly against impact criteria.
  • Counterbalance with: S3 Deployment frequency
  • Common mistake: Claiming credit for incident reduction that came from unrelated infra work — causation, not correlation; tag root causes.
A5 Suggestion acceptance rate   Level 1 · start now Blue Money

1 · What it is

% of AI code suggestions developers accept. Diagnostic only — the most misleading number in the GenAI conversation if used as 'productivity'.
10-second example: Copilot dashboard shows Nova at 31% acceptance — perfectly normal; panic is warranted near 0% (broken adoption) or near 100% (blind trust).

2 · How to capture it Example stack: Account level · Copilot metrics API/dashboard · Owner: Engineering Manager · Monthly · Effort: ~5 min

  1. Read monthly from the Copilot usage dashboard or metrics API. No setup.
  2. Record per team, alongside churn (Q5) once available.
  3. Do not set targets on it. Ever.

3 · Baseline it

First 3 months: Nova 31%, Atlas 24%, Phoenix 35%, Vega 28% — all in the healthy 20-40% band.

4 · Track improvements

M1M3M6Read
Nova acceptance31%33%34%Stable — fine
Hypothetical team X8%7%6%Broken adoption → investigate

Use it like a smoke detector, not a speedometer. Team X's 7% means licences are wasted — run enablement. A team at 85% with rising churn means suggestions are being accepted uncritically — strengthen review. In both cases the number prompts a question; it never answers one.

5 · What it means in each execution model

T&MNever show a client. Internal enablement signal only.
Managed ServicesSame.
Fixed PriceSame.
Outcome-BasedSame.

If a client's procurement team asks for it, provide it with the explicit caveat that it is an adoption signal, not a value measure — and offer A3 instead.

6 · Blue Money — and the path to Green

Classification: Blue — never converts.

None. Explicitly barred from ROI decks and business cases. Its only legitimate uses: spotting broken adoption and spotting blind trust. Trigger — <20% or >70% sustained for 2 months → enablement/review-practice check; Owner — Engineering Manager; Measure — the follow-up action, not the rate itself.

🗣 Say it to the client: Do not present. If pressed: “We track it operationally, but industry evidence shows it doesn't correlate with delivered value — here's what does.” (show S1/A3)

7 · Watch-outs

  • Gaming risk: Trivially gameable (accept-then-delete). This is WHY it's banned from value claims.
  • Counterbalance with: Q5 Code churn
  • Common mistake: Vendor decks lead with it because it always looks good. Don't import their slide into yours.

Level 2 — honest speed & people signals Months 2-3

S1 Cycle time (first commit → production)   Level 2 · light automation Green Money

1 · What it is

Elapsed time from the first commit on a change until it runs in production. The single most honest speed signal — it includes coding, review, testing and deploy, which is where GenAI gains go to die.
10-second example: first commit Tuesday 10:00, deployed Friday 16:00 → 4.25 days.

2 · How to capture it Example stack: Team Nova (T&M · GitHub + Actions) · Owner: Tech Lead + DevOps · Monthly · Effort: Med (30 min manual → automated)

  1. Start manual: sample 25 changes/month; for each, first-commit timestamp (PR commits tab) → prod deploy timestamp (Actions run). Median them.
  2. Automate in month 2-3: a ~50-line script joining the PR API with the deployments API; or built-in analytics (ADO Lead/Cycle time widgets give this free).
  3. Third-party (LinearB/Swarmia-class) only if the client approves the data access.

3 · Baseline it

Nova pre-GenAI: median 5.2 days (p75: 9 days — track p75 too; the median hides the painful tail).

4 · Track improvements

BaselineQ1Q2Read
Median5.2d5.1d4.1d-21% by Q2 ✓
p759.0d9.2d6.8dTail shrinking ✓
Defect density (pair)3.63.83.5Held ✓

Q1 was flat despite Copilot — because review was the constraint (see S5). After fixing the review bottleneck, Q2 shows the genuine end-to-end gain. This sequencing — tool, then bottleneck, then gain — is the realistic story to tell leadership, and far more credible than instant miracles.

5 · What it means in each execution model

T&MFreed hours are only Green if redeployed to billable work: Nova's 21% ≈ 1.5 verified hrs/dev/week ≈ 620 hrs/yr ≈ $31K billable capacity — IF the account manager books it. Unbooked, it leaks.
Managed ServicesFaster change delivery = faster fix flow = better SLA posture at same headcount.
Fixed Price21% faster on the critical path = earlier milestones or absorbed scope changes = protected margin.
Outcome-BasedShorter cycle = faster path to each contracted milestone payment.

6 · Green Money — and the path to Green

Classification: Green — anchor metric.

This is the anchor for every credible speed claim. Convert the measured delta (never the survey delta) into capacity: verified hrs/dev/week × devs × weeks × rate. Then make the leakage decision explicit — Redeploy (book freed hours to billable/backlog), Resize (absorb growth without hiring), or Reinvest (build the next offering). Unallocated savings evaporate into 'comfort'. Trigger — quarterly capacity-allocation decision on the measured delta; Owner — Delivery Manager; Measure — booked hours / avoided requisitions, with Finance.

🗣 Say it to the client: “Delivery is measurably 21% faster end-to-end — and here is exactly where those regained hours went: [feature X shipped early / scope Y absorbed at no change cost].”

7 · Watch-outs

  • Gaming risk: Cherry-picking easy changes into the sample — sample systematically (every Nth).
  • Counterbalance with: Q3 Defect density + Q1 CFR
  • Common mistake: Reporting mean instead of median (one 40-day outlier wrecks it); baselining after rollout began.

  3. Pick ONE of S1/S2 as your primary number internally to avoid double-reporting; keep the other for benchmarking.

Identical capacity conversion as S1 — never count it twice. Trigger/Owner/Measure — shared with S1.

🗣 Say it to the client: “Against the DORA industry benchmark, this team moved from median to the high-performing band in two quarters.”

  • Counterbalance with: Q1 CFR

Q2 Rework rate   Level 2 · light automation Green Money

1 · What it is

% of deployments that are fixes to work previously declared done — reverts, hotfixes, patches. The earliest instability alarm: it degrades before CFR and incidents do.
10-second example: of Phoenix's 20 deployments in June, 3 were labeled hotfix/revert → rework = 15%.

2 · How to capture it Example stack: Team Phoenix (Fixed Price · GitLab) · Owner: Tech Lead · Monthly · Effort: ~15 min after setup

  1. Team convention (10-min setup): every fix MR gets a label — hotfix, revert, or patch.
  2. Monthly: labeled MRs deployed ÷ total deployments. Saved GitLab search per label.
  3. Reconcile occasionally with git: git log --grep='revert' --oneline --since='1 month ago' catches unlabeled reverts.

3 · Baseline it

Phoenix pre-GenAI: 6% of deployment effort was rework.

4 · Track improvements

BaselineQ1Q2Read
Rework rate6%9%5.5%Ramp bump, then better
PR throughput (pair)3.14.24.6Volume up

The Q1 spike is AI code that "looked done" being merged faster than it was verified. Phoenix's fix: an AI-code checklist in review (edge cases, error handling, integration points) + mandatory tests for AI-heavy MRs. Q2 beat baseline. Rising rework alongside rising throughput is THE early paradox signature — catch it here, months before it reaches CFR or the client.

5 · What it means in each execution model

T&MRework hours are hours the client pays for twice — awkward conversations at renewal.
Managed ServicesLeading indicator of the incident wave that triggers penalties next quarter.
Fixed PriceThe purest margin drain: rework is unpaid work on a fixed budget.
Outcome-BasedRework consumes the capacity needed to hit contracted milestones.

6 · Green Money — and the path to Green

Classification: Green — outcome metric.

Convert via returned hours: (baseline − actual rework %) × deployment effort × rate. Phoenix: 0.5pt improvement on ~1,600 deploy-effort hrs/qtr ≈ 8 hrs/qtr — small alone, but the real value is the avoided Q1-pattern: unchecked, 9% rework compounds into failed milestones. The failure mode reads the opposite way: feature share FALLING during a GenAI rollout means AI-generated volume is creating downstream defect work faster than it creates features (the paradox, seen from the portfolio level).

5 · What it means in each execution model

T&MMore feature share = more of the client's spend visibly building their product — renewal gold.
Managed ServicesShrinking defect/toil share at fixed fee = margin expansion, quantified.
Fixed PriceFeature share is where the bid's scope actually gets delivered.
Outcome-BasedCapacity on outcomes vs overhead — often reported in QBRs.

6 · Green Money — and the path to Green

Classification: Green — capacity mix.

Points shifted × team cost = capacity redirected to value creation — a Finance-friendly framing because it's the same money doing more valuable work. Trigger — feature share drops 3+ pts → root-cause into defect/debt inflow; Owner — PMO; Measure — the four-way mix, quarterly, co-read with Finance.

🗣 Say it to the client: “Six points more of your spend now builds new capability instead of fixing old problems — that's ~$54K/yr of capacity redirected to your roadmap, at zero additional cost.”

7 · Watch-outs

  • Gaming risk: Misclassifying fixes as features. Monthly sample audit; classification owned by PMO, not sprint-pressured devs.
  • Counterbalance with: Q7 Tech debt ratio (don't starve debt work to inflate feature share!)
  • Common mistake: Chasing 100% features — healthy teams deliberately spend 15-25% on debt/risk; the goal is a deliberate mix, not a maximal one.
I4 Hard savings realized (Green Money ledger)   Level 3 · advanced Green Money

1 · What it is

The Finance-recognized cash events GenAI actually caused: decommissioned tools, avoided requisitions, reduced incident cost, SLA value. Not a calculation — a LEDGER of evidenced events. This is the sheet the CFO reads first.
10-second example: "Test-data tool decommissioned 30 Jun, contract #4711, $30K/yr — replaced by AI-generated test data. Evidence: cancellation notice. Finance sign-off: 12 Jul."

2 · How to capture it Example stack: Account level · Finance + Delivery Managers · Owner: Delivery Manager + Finance · Quarterly · Effort: Med (the discipline, not the math)

  1. Maintain the ledger in the ROI Blue-to-Green sheet: date, event, $ amount, evidence link, causation note, Finance sign-off.
  2. Qualifying events ONLY: cancelled contract/license · closed or avoided requisition (req number) · measured incident-cost reduction (I2 × agreed rate) · SLA bonus/penalty delta.
  3. Each entry needs Finance sign-off BEFORE it appears in any client or leadership material.
  4. Everything else (hours saved, capacity freed) stays in the discounted-soft-savings section — never in this ledger.

3 · Baseline it

Ledger starts at $0 on rollout day. Year-1 example entries: test-tool decommission $30K · one contractor requisition closed, AI absorbed the workload $120K · incident reduction (I2, Finance-agreed model) $48K · SLA penalties avoided $25K → $223K evidenced Green Money vs $70.6K program cost.

4 · Track improvements

Q1Q2Q3Q4
Ledger total (cum.)$0$30K$150K$223K
Program cost (cum.)$18K$35K$53K$71K

Q1 at $0 is CORRECT and should be presented as such — hard events take quarters to mature, and a credible $0 beats a fabricated number. The crossover quarter (Q2-Q3 here) is the moment the program is self-evidently paying for itself in CFO terms. The ratio of ledger to claimed-soft-savings is your credibility ratio — keep it above ~30% by year end or re-scope the story.

5 · What it means in each execution model

T&MRequisitions avoided while revenue held = margin; freed capacity booked to new billable work = the ledger's revenue side.
Managed ServicesPenalties avoided + clients absorbed without headcount — both auditable.
Fixed PriceDelivered under budgeted hours → margin delta on the project P&L (track it or it silently disappears into 'came in fine').
Outcome-BasedBonus clauses triggered + penalty clauses avoided — literally contractual cash.

6 · Green Money — and the path to Green

Classification: Green — the destination.

This IS Green Money; nothing to convert. The discipline: Trigger — every Blue metric improvement prompts the question 'what cash event can this now cause?' logged as a candidate; Owner — named Delivery Manager per candidate (not 'the team'); Measure — the signed ledger, quarterly, with a 6-month verification date on every claim.

🗣 Say it to the client: “Beyond the delivery improvements, here are the audited cash events: $223K in year one against a $71K program cost — every line has an invoice, a requisition number, or your own SLA schedule behind it.”

7 · Watch-outs

  • Gaming risk: Claiming savings that would have happened anyway. Causation note is mandatory per entry; Finance is the gatekeeper.
  • Counterbalance with: E1 DevEx (savings extracted by burnout reverse themselves)
  • Common mistake: Presenting the projected version of this ledger. It is a record of what HAPPENED — projections live in the ROI calculator, clearly labeled.

Closing: the three questions every metric must survive

  • What specific Green Money? Not "improved productivity" — a dollar amount on a P&L line, a requisition number, an SLA schedule.
  • Who is personally accountable? A name, not a team.
  • How will we know in 6 months? A dated, measurable verification — not "we'll monitor it".

If a proposed metric or claim can't answer all three, it can still be useful — as a diagnostic. It just doesn't belong in front of the client's CFO yet.
